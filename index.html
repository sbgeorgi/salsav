Semi-arid Lab for Scaling Agrivoltaics

Our lab researches and implements agrivoltaic systems to create resilient food, energy, and water solutions in arid regions. We scale this work from local test plots to global partnerships, building a data-driven roadmap for a sustainable future.

The Agrivoltaics Revolution

Pioneered by researchers like Greg Barron-Gafford at the University of Arizona, agrivoltaics is a symbiotic system for a hotter, drier world—boosting the resilience of our food, water, and energy systems. Scroll to see how.

Optimized Solar Panels

Elevated solar arrays create a cooler microclimate. Research by Barron-Gafford's team shows this partial shade not only protects crops but also makes the panels themselves more efficient by mitigating extreme heat.

Engineered Structure

The custom framework is raised high enough for modern farm equipment, enabling continued agricultural practices. This design is crucial for the real-world application and economic viability demonstrated in Arizona research sites.

Increased Crop Yield

In the Sonoran Desert, Barron-Gafford's studies found that crops like chiltepin peppers produced three times more fruit, and tomatoes doubled their yield under the protective shade—all while using significantly less water.

Dual-Use Energy

The cooler, more efficient panels generate clean, renewable electricity. This power can be used on-farm to power irrigation, or sold to the grid, creating a vital new revenue stream for farmers and strengthening local energy independence.

Radical Water Savings

The panel canopy is a powerful tool for water conservation. By reducing sun exposure and wind, soil moisture is preserved, cutting water loss from evaporation. It's a critical advantage in arid regions and a cornerstone of the research.

Ecosystem Regeneration

Agrivoltaic sites can become havens for biodiversity. By planting native, flowering vegetation in and around the arrays, these systems support vital pollinator populations, improve soil health, and create a more resilient, integrated landscape.

The Food-Energy-Water Nexus

Agrivoltaics is a data-driven solution, proven in harsh environments, that turns a single plot of land into a powerhouse of sustainable production. A vision for the future of agriculture, realized today.

Research in Agrivoltaics Systems

01. Ecophysiology

The Plant-Panel Symbiosis

The partial shade of solar panels creates a unique growing environment for crops. How does the AV environment affect crop physiology, water use, phenology and yield?
02. Scaling Up

From Kilowatts to Megawatts

Taking proven benefits from test plots to utility-scale requires robust engineering and economic models. We analyze system designs and financial viability to create a clear roadmap for profitable agrivoltaic projects.
03. Scaling Out

Adapting to Diverse Contexts

An agrivoltaic solution for Arizona's desert differs from one for Colorado's pastures. Our research spans multiple climates and crop types to develop adaptable, resilient models that work worldwide.

Our Global SALSA Learning Labs

From the drylands of Arizona to equatorial Kenya, our research spans diverse climates to create adaptable, resilient agrivoltaic models.

Arizona, USA

Biosphere 2 Laboratory

The pioneering site where agrivoltaics in drylands was born, proving a 'win-win-win' for food, water, and energy.
Colorado, USA

Jack’s Solar Garden

A nationally-recognized showcase focusing on microclimate benefits and improving pasture quality for livestock grazing.
Makueni County, Kenya

Community Agrivoltaics

Extreme Climate Research

Ben Guerir, Morocco

Green Energy Park

A regional hub for West Africa, focusing on water-saving tech, battery storage, and increasing solar output via crop cooling.
Mexico City, Mexico
